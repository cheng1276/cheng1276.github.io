Built 2026-10-06 under /home/claude/research/PROTOCOL.md. Every quote below was read in this session. This is an evidence file only; it contains no patient-facing Chinese text.

**How to read this file (method notes)**
- Primary source is **S1, the 2026 International Guideline** (PMID 41649409). It **supersedes** the 2022 EAACI/GA²LEN/EuroGuiDerm/APAAACI guideline (S1a) and the 2018 version (S1b). Text came from PMC full text.
- **Extraction pitfall check (S1):** the PMC extraction drops bold or italic words. In S1's recommendation boxes the verb is lost ("Wethat …", "Weaiming …"). Direction and strength were therefore taken only from the surviving arrow symbols (↑↑ / ↑ / 0 / ↓ / ↓↓), using S1 Table 4 and the Methods text ("a strong recommendation is made (i.e., “We recommend…” and “↑↑” in dark green)"). Every narrative S1 quote used here was checked for glued words. Where a word is missing, the Notes column says so. No for/against direction rests on a glued sentence.
- **Boxes missing from S1:** S1's treatment recommendation boxes (for example on antihistamine choice) are absent from the extracted text. None of S1's lifestyle statements (trigger avoidance, diet, diaries, daily antihistamines) sit in a graded box in the accessible text; they are narrative. They are labelled "Narrative text" below.
- **WebFetch sources (S2, S3, S6, part of S7):** WebFetch passes pages through a summarizer that limits each quote to about 125 characters. For these sources I re-fetched every quote and used only wording that came back the same in at least two passes. Where only one pass returned a quote, the Notes say so.
- **Not used for claims:** dosing of prescription drugs is not recorded. Dose figures that appear inside quotes were removed and marked "[dose omitted]".

## E. Could not verify / open questions

1. **TDA 2021 (S4) full text was not accessible.** ScienceDirect and jfma-online are blocked by robots.txt; the NTU repository and yxj.org.cn show the abstract only; there is no PMC version. Unknown: what TDA says on triggers, diet, NSAIDs, daily antihistamines, UAS7, skin care or emergencies, and the consensus percentage for each. **Suggested fix:** the physician or TDA members obtain the PDF.
2. **2014 US parameter (S2):** the online-only numbered Summary Statements and their **strength grades** were not accessed (the JACI page needed a permission that was not granted; the AAAAI PDF is the print executive summary). S2 claims are labelled "Strength not found in accessible text".
3. **2026 US JTFPP guideline (S3) is a draft** (member comment period ended 30 June 2026). No journal publication was found by 2026-10-06. Wording, strength and content may change; check for the final version. One supplementary sentence had unclear context and was not used: "If attacks, however, are frequent (e.g. weekly), daily dosing may be more effective than solely on-demand dosing." The draft's "supportive care" was not defined in the text I retrieved.
4. **The WebFetch summarizer caps quotes at about 125 characters.** S2, S3, S6 and part of S7 were read this way. Quotes were re-checked across passes, and single-pass items are labelled. A few minor variants were seen (for example "Subjects" vs "Patients" in C51). Before publishing, a human should check key S2 and S6 quotes against the PDFs.
5. **Size of the NSAID effect:** S1 says "up to one‐fourth"; S8 (WAO 2025) says "up to 30%". Unresolved.
6. **Heat and alcohol as CSU aggravators:** heat is supported only by S2 (2014, cautious "might"), and alcohol only by S6 (2015) plus the S3 draft. **Neither appears in S1 (2026).** Use cautious wording.
7. **Skin care (cool environment, moisturisers or emollients, loose clothing):** **no Tier-1 or Tier-2 source found** that recommends moisturisers or a cool environment for urticaria. Only BSACI's cooling menthol lotion (C88) and the US "tight clothing might exacerbate" statement (C24) were found. S1's mention of "loose‐fitting clothing" (C53) describes dermographism and is not advice.
8. **WAO 2020 anaphylaxis criteria (C85):** criterion 1's required additional-organ sub-items were not retrieved word for word (the figure is missing from PMC and the WebFetch result was partial).
9. **ACE inhibitors:** BSACI (S6) says they are "contraindicated in individuals with a history of angioedema with or without weals". S1 has no equivalent statement for CSU patients with angioedema; it covers ACE inhibitors only as a differential diagnosis for angioedema without wheals. The BSACI strength grade could not be pinned to a sentence. **Physician judgement needed** on how to word this.
10. **CIndU consensus (S18, Magerl 2016):** full text not accessible (Wiley returned 403). Subtype-specific patient advice, such as cold-water or swimming precautions, could not be checked there. The cold-urticaria systemic-reaction warning (C51) rests on S2 alone.
11. **Dropped words in S1** that were not relied on for direction: the word after "In most types of" (C03; context implies CIndU) and the italic organism name in §5.2.3 (not used).
12. **S1's reference lists are not in the extracted text.** I could not confirm which RCTs support the daily-antihistamine advice (S11 was found independently) or which studies support the stress and diet statements.
13. **Taiwan emergency number and local services** are not in any source; take them from an official Taiwanese source if needed.
14. **Vaccination:** no urticaria guideline makes a recommendation about non-COVID vaccines for CU patients.
15. **PMCID checks:** `convert_article_ids` was rate-limited on 4 attempts. PMCIDs were therefore taken from `get_article_metadata` and confirmed with `find_related_articles` (pubmed_pmc), which returned PMC links for S1, S7, S8 and S15 and none for the others.
