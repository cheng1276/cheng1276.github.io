## Reading notes (please read before using any row)

- **PMC-extracted quotes (access "full text (PMC)")**: the extraction tool removes citation numbers, which leaves empty brackets such as "()" or "(,)". They are reproduced as extracted. Non-standard spaces in the PMC text (non-breaking and thin spaces, e.g. "25 110", "360 μmol/L") were typed as ordinary spaces; no other character was changed. The tool also drops formatted (bold/italic/superscript) words. **In the 2020 ACR gout guideline it dropped "against" from the asymptomatic-hyperuricaemia recommendation** ("conditionally recommendedin"). The direction was confirmed from the co-published version (S33, two separate reads) and is recorded only in its confirmed form (C48). Other glued words seen in this session are listed in Section E; none of them is inside a recorded quote unless the Notes say so.
- **WebFetch-derived quotes (access "full text via WebFetch")**: these pages (publisher HTML, society PDFs, NICE pages, the Deep Blue repository PDF, J-STAGE) were read through a fetch tool that passes the page through a summarising model, which refuses quotes longer than about 125 characters. Long sentences were therefore requested in consecutive short segments; segments are shown in separate quotation marks. The key sentences were requested twice with differently worded prompts and matched both times (stated in Notes). **Check every WebFetch-derived quote character by character against the original document before publication** (list in Section E).
- **Machine check**: every quote whose source text was saved in this session (PMC full text or PubMed abstract) was checked by script for an exact match after collapsing whitespace. WebFetch quotes could not be machine-checked.
- **Confidence** (same convention as the existing gout.md): High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session. Medium = Tier-2, or Tier-1 abstract only, or a Tier-1 sentence obtained from a single WebFetch read. Low = secondary source, indirect evidence, or **any Tier-3 single study** (a Low row is quoted accurately but sits in the lowest evidence tier). Choosing Wisely lists are treated as Tier-1 professional-society statements, with their publication status stated in Section A.
- **Medication-adjacent / clinician-level** rows record a guideline's position on whether drug treatment is recommended. They are not patient instructions and contain no dosing.
- **Titres are not comparable across studies.** Studies used different substrates, kits, screening dilutions (1:40, 1:80, 1:100, 1:160, 1:320) and positivity rules, and different "healthy" populations (blood donors, hospital workers, health-check attendees, national survey samples that are not screened for disease). Each row states the dilution and population.
- Abbreviations: ANA = antinuclear antibody; IIF/IIFA = indirect immunofluorescence (assay); HEp-2 = the human cell line used as substrate; SARD = systemic autoimmune rheumatic disease; CTD = connective tissue disease; RF = rheumatoid factor; ACPA = anti-citrullinated protein antibody (anti-CCP is the usual test); SU/SUA = serum urate / serum uric acid; ULT/ULA = urate-lowering therapy / agent; MSU = monosodium urate; CKD = chronic kidney disease; CVD = cardiovascular disease; ULN = upper limit of normal.

## E. Could not verify / open questions / conflicts

**Conflicts between sources (flagged for the page author)**
1. **Drug treatment of asymptomatic hyperuricaemia.** ACR 2020: conditional recommendation **against** starting ULT, certainty high, including people with CKD, CVD, urolithiasis or hypertension (C48–C50). Japanese guideline 3rd ed. (secondary source only): consider ULA at SUA ≥9 mg/dL, or ≥8 mg/dL with complications, after lifestyle modification; ULA conditionally recommended in hyperuricaemia with kidney injury (certainty B) (C58, C59). Chinese 2024 (endocrinology society): ULT should start at SU ≥9 mg/dL or with cardiovascular, metabolic or kidney comorbidities (C61). Korean 2023: no recommendation for CKD 3–4 (benefit/harm unclear) (C63). EULAR 2018/2016: no recommendation on asymptomatic hyperuricaemia (C52). HKSR 2023: none (C64). APLAR 2021: 2 statements, content unread (C66). Taiwan 2018: abstract silent/ambiguous (C65). The RCT and Cochrane evidence on kidney protection is negative or very uncertain (C67–C69).
2. **Kidney protection by ULT**: Japanese guideline (conditional for, certainty B) vs ACR 2020 (against, including CKD), Korean (no recommendation), CKD-FIX and PERL (no benefit), Cochrane 2017 (very uncertain).
3. **RF and age**: Nielsen 2012 (RF constant across ages 20–100; C37) vs Oyeyinka 1995 (higher positivity over 65; C43) and the common belief that RF rises with age. No Asian data found.
4. **Referral for positive RF without symptoms**: Nielsen 2012 authors suggest referral even without joint symptoms (C40); CRA 2025 advises against ordering RF/ACPA unless CSA or arthritis (C30); EULAR/NICE referral triggers are based on joint swelling/synovitis (C45, C46). No guideline recommending referral for asymptomatic RF positivity was found.
5. **Routine ANA testing in healthy people**: Ge 2022 authors: "Thus, ANA testing should be routine for healthy people" (S22, abstract); Li 2019 authors say their study "is conducive to promoting the popularization of antinuclear antibody detection" (S17, Conclusion). Both contradict ACR/ACP/CRA Choosing Wisely statements (C25–C31). These are single-centre author opinions.
6. **DFS pattern**: ICAP (consensus, not systematic review) describes AC-2 as commonly found in healthy people (C22); the Hung 2026 meta-analysis found overall anti-DFS70 not significantly more common in healthy people than in SLE; only monospecific anti-DFS70 was (C23).
7. **ANA and age**: Tan 1997 found no difference across ages 20–60 (C1); Satoh 2012 found prevalence increased with age (non-linear) (C3); Guo 2014 found positive correlation with age (C9).
8. **5-year gout risk at SU >9 mg/dl**: ACR 2020 says 20% (C49); EULAR 2018 and Campion 1987 say 22% (C52, C57). Probably rounding of the same data; not verified through the reference lists.
9. **ACR 2020 threshold**: text "≥6.8 mg/dl" vs Table 1 ">6.8 mg/dl" (C47).
