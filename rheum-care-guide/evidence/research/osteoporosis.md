## Reading notes (please read before using any row)

- **Scope boundary: glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (GIOP) is NOT covered here.** It is already on the common page (common.md section B7, rows C54–C61 and C92; ACR 2022, ACR 2017, ECTS 2024). NOGG 2024 (S1) also contains GIOP recommendations; they were deliberately not recorded. Link to the common page for GIOP.
- **Superseded / retired sources found in this session.** NOGG 2022 (the version named in the addendum) is superseded by **NOGG 2024** (S1, "a minor update of the 2021 guideline"). The **Endocrine Society 2012 "Osteoporosis in Men" guideline was retired on 16 October 2025** (S10; not used). USPSTF 2025 osteoporosis screening and USPSTF 2024 falls statements supersede their 2018 versions. The USPSTF 2018 vitamin D/calcium statement is still the final version, but the USPSTF page says an update is in progress (draft not read). The Taiwan Osteoporosis Association (TOA) has a **2025** Chinese guideline (S28) that could not be read (robots.txt); the readable TOA edition is 2021 (S26).
- **PMC-extracted quotes (access "full text (PMC)")**: the tool strips citation numbers, leaving empty brackets such as "[]" or "[,]", reproduced as extracted. It also drops formatted words. In this session it dropped words from **BHOF 2022 (S3)** in four places: "intakebe achieved" (true text "cannot be achieved"), "ofcalcium" twice (true text "of supplemental calcium" and "of elemental calcium"), and "havefound" (true text "**have not found**", a reversal of meaning). All four were restored from the Springer PDF via WebFetch (Notes give details). In **Strong, Steady and Straight (S16)** the Box 1 sub-headings lost "do not have / have" ("osteoporosisvertebral fractures"); the mapping was confirmed from the abstract and the bjsm.bmj.com page. Every PMC passage quoted was screened with a glued-word detector (rare tokens splittable into two common words); remaining hits were false positives or missing spaces only (e.g., "osteoporosisregardless", "fracture,highlighting"), noted where they occur.
- **WebFetch-derived quotes (access "full text via WebFetch")**: AACE 2020 (S6; AACE-hosted PDF), TOA 2021/2020 (S26/S27; hospital-hosted PDFs), HPA/MOHW 2014 (S29), the USPSTF web page (S14), the Springer PDF of S3 and the bjsm.bmj.com page of S16. WebFetch passes text through a summarising model that refuses quotes over about 125 characters, so long sentences were returned in consecutive segments; segments are reproduced inside one pair of quotation marks separated by "" "". Key quotes were re-read and matched (stated in Notes). **Check WebFetch-derived quotes character by character against the original before publication.**
- **Confidence**: High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session. Medium = Tier-2; Tier-1 abstract only; a Tier-1 consensus without formal grading; or a Taiwanese guideline edition that may be superseded. Low = secondary source, indirect evidence, or any Tier-3 single study.
- **Medication-adjacent** rows are patient-behaviour messages only. No prescription-drug dosing is recorded (supplement amounts for calcium and vitamin D are recorded because guidelines state them as nutrition targets).
- Abbreviations: BMD = bone mineral density; DXA = dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry; VF = vertebral fracture; VFA = vertebral fracture assessment; FLS = fracture liaison service; ONJ/MRONJ = (medication-related) osteonecrosis of the jaw; AFF = atypical femoral fracture; FRIDs = fall-risk-increasing drugs; RDA/RNI/DRI = dietary reference values; GPS = good practice statement; [E]/[C] in S16 = evidence-based / expert consensus; NHMRC grades A–D in S5; AACE Grade A–D with best evidence level (BEL) in S6; WFG grades 1A–2C/E in S11 (1 = benefits clearly outweigh undesirable effects; A–C = certainty; E = expert consensus).

## E. Could not verify / open questions / conflicts

1. **SUPERSEDED: NOGG 2022 → NOGG 2024.** The addendum lists NOGG 2022 (PMID 35378630). NOGG 2024 (S1, PMID 40921943) replaces it ("a minor update of the 2021 guideline"). The 2022 text was not read in full, so differences in lifestyle wording between the two were not compared.
2. **RETIRED: Endocrine Society 2012 "Osteoporosis in Men".** According to the WebFetch summary of the OUP notice (single read; the retirement date was given in the tool's own words, not as a verbatim quote), the guideline was retired on 16 October 2025. Confirm on the journal page before citing. No replacement found. The status of the Endocrine Society 2019/2020 postmenopausal guideline (S9) was not confirmed; only its abstract principle (iii) is used.
3. **USPSTF 2018 vitamin D/calcium statement (S14)**: still final, but "This topic is being updated" (draft for falls and fractures not read). Re-check before publishing.
4. **Taiwanese guideline not fully verified.** S28 (2025) unread (robots.txt; not circumvented). S25 (2022 English update) abstract only. S26 (2021) was read via WebFetch, which returned short verbatim segments; page numbers were not available. Before publishing, compare C14, C31, C41, C68, C71 with the 2025 edition.
5. **CONFLICT: calcium targets.** NOGG minimum 700 mg (UK RNI); ESCEO/IOF RNI 800–1000 mg; BHOF 1000 mg (men 50–70) / 1200 mg (women ≥51, men ≥71); Osteoporosis Canada RDA 1000/1200 mg; AACE 1200 mg (women ≥50); Asia-Pacific men 1000–1200 mg; RACGP 1000 mg / 1300 mg (women >50, men >70); TOA 2021 at least 1200 mg (citing NOF/IOF); Taiwan HPA DRI 1000 mg for adults (2014). Suggestion for editors: use a range that matches the Taiwanese guideline and DRI once the 2025 TOA edition and current DRI are checked; state "mainly from food".
6. **CONFLICT: vitamin D amounts.** NOGG ≥800 IU/day for those with insufficiency or risk factors (800–2000 IU "may be appropriate", Evidence level IV); BHOF 800–1000 units; AACE 1000–2000 IU; Osteoporosis Canada follows Health Canada (600/800 IU RDA, 400 IU supplement over 50); ESCEO/IOF 800 IU RNI; Asia-Pacific men ≥800 IU; TOA 2021 800–1000 IU; Endocrine Society 2024 suggests against empiric supplementation above the DRI in healthy adults <75 but excludes people "with established indications" (whether osteoporosis is such an indication was not confirmed — abstract only). Serum 25(OH)D targets are clinician-level and also differ (AACE ≥30 ng/mL; RACGP >50 nmol/L); do not print.
7. **CONFLICT: alcohol.** NOGG and AACE: ≤2 units/day; BHOF: more than 2 drinks/day (women) or 3 (men) may be detrimental, moderate intake "no known negative effect"; TOA 2021: "戒酒" (stop alcohol). Unit/drink sizes are not defined in the texts read. The common page (GIOP) uses ACR 2022 "≤2 servings a day". Do not convert to grams without a source.
8. **CONFLICT: back bracing after vertebral fracture.** NOGG: routine bracing "cannot be recommended" (Ia). BHOF: acute treatment "includes ... bracing (for 2 to 6 weeks)". Both describe low-quality evidence for 6-month bracing.
9. **CONFLICT / population difference: impact exercise and jumping.** RACGP Rec 17 (general): ≥50 moderate impacts most days. SSS: moderate impact only for people without vertebral fractures; people with vertebral fractures up to brisk walking. Osteoporosis Canada: impact only if appropriate; uncertain at high fracture risk. BHOF lists jumping rope, running/jogging (with low spinal BMD), golf, tennis and bowling (with twisting) as potentially injurious, yet also lists jogging and tennis as weight-bearing examples (internal tension). HPA 2014 (general population) recommends jumping rope and jogging.
10. **CONFLICT: kidney stones.** USPSTF/Kahwati (calcium + vitamin D increases stones; calcium alone not, in small RCTs), NOGG, RACGP, ESCEO/IOF and BHOF (supplemental calcium above 1200–1500 mg in at-risk people) vs Ma 2024 umbrella review ("made no difference or even reduced the risk"). Lin 2020: dietary calcium protective; supplements raised risk in observational data, and co-supplementation in RCTs. TOA 2021 attributes kidney-stone risk to total intake above 1200–1500 mg, not specifically supplements.
11. **CONFLICT: calcium and cardiovascular risk.** TOA 2021: intake above 1200–1500 mg "增加...心血管疾病風險" (increases cardiovascular risk). NOGG: supplements may increase kidney stones "but not the incidence of cardiovascular disease or cancer" (Ia). BHOF: a large meta-analysis "found no evidence that calcium with or without vitamin D increased cardiovascular disease". ESCEO/IOF: increased cardiovascular risk "is not convincingly supported by current evidence". RACGP: "not all studies support this conclusion". Not a lifestyle claim row; flagged for editors.
12. **CONFLICT: BMD testing in men.** USPSTF 2025: insufficient evidence (I). BHOF, TOA 2021 and the Asia-Pacific men's consensus: test men ≥70 (and younger men with risk factors). RACGP: no population screening, case finding. Osteoporosis Canada: targeted approach, most testing from age 70.
13. **CONFLICT (minor): exercise after vertebral fracture.** NOGG (Strong) and SSS ([C]) recommend back-extensor/strengthening exercise; Cochrane (S20) finds insufficient evidence on fractures/falls and only a small, not clinically important functional gain. Recommendations rely partly on consensus.
14. **DROPPED WORDS (S3, BHOF PMC text), all confirmed from the Springer PDF via WebFetch:** "intakebe achieved" → "cannot be achieved"; "intake ofcalcium above 1200 to 1500 mg/day" → "intake of supplemental calcium above 1200 to 1500 mg/day"; "milligrams ofcalcium" → "milligrams of elemental calcium" (not used as a claim); "that havefound supplemental vitamin D to be effective" → "that have not found supplemental vitamin D to be effective" (re-read word by word; the meaning is reversed in the PMC text). Another broken BHOF sentence ("fall incidence was 74%in the home-based exercise group than ...") was not used.
15. **DROPPED WORDS (S16, SSS PMC text):** Box 1 sub-headings "For people with osteoporosisvertebral fractures or multiple low-trauma fractures" (twice). Mapping confirmed from the abstract and the bjsm.bmj.com page (single WebFetch read; sub-heading wording may be paraphrased by the tool).
