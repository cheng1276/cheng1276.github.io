## Topic-specific scope

### pregnancy — 懷孕、哺乳與避孕 (all rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases, women and men)
The physician wants this page written MORE cautiously than the rest of the site because it touches
medicines: the site will NOT name individual drugs. Collect the patient-level principles that guidelines
state, for example:
- family planning discussed early and repeatedly; most people with RMDs can have a successful pregnancy;
- planning conception for a period of low disease activity / quiescence (record any stated duration) and
  while taking pregnancy-compatible medicines; pre-pregnancy counselling;
- reliable contraception when pregnancy is not planned, especially with disease activity or medicines
  that can harm a fetus; how the choice of method depends on patient factors such as antiphospholipid
  antibodies (record method classes as stated, e.g. IUD, implant, estrogen-containing methods; these
  are not individual drug names, but mark each such claim "method class");
- medicines before and during pregnancy: the general principles only (some medicines must be stopped a
  certain time before conception; others can or should be continued; do not stop on your own; discuss
  before trying). You MAY record drug-specific statements as background, but mark every such claim
  "drug-specific — not for site text" and extract the generic principle in a separate claim;
- men: whether paternal medicines matter and that men should discuss medicines before conception (record
  what guidelines say, generic principle separately from drug-specific details);
- monitoring during pregnancy and after delivery: joint care with obstetrics / maternal-fetal medicine,
  more frequent visits, antibody testing before pregnancy (antiphospholipid antibodies, anti-Ro/SSA and
  anti-La/SSB — record in which diseases), post-partum flare risk and follow-up;
- breastfeeding: whether it is encouraged; the generic principle about medicines while breastfeeding;
- infants exposed in utero to biologic medicines: live vaccines (e.g. rotavirus, BCG) — what ACR 2020 /
  ACR 2022 vaccination / EULAR say, and ANY Taiwan CDC (疾管署) guidance on 卡介苗 for infants whose
  mothers used biologics during pregnancy (Taiwan gives BCG in infancy, so this matters);
- fertility: age, smoking, alcohol; fertility preservation before gonadotoxic treatment (generic);
  assisted reproduction (discuss with the rheumatologist beforehand, disease stable); menopause hormone
  therapy only if a source gives a patient-level message;
- myths Taiwanese patients hold, if a source addresses them: "rheumatic disease means you cannot have
  children", "you must stop all medicines when pregnant", "you cannot breastfeed while taking medicine",
  "a father's medicines do not matter", "pregnancy always improves the disease" (record disease-specific
  statements, e.g. RA vs SLE, with sources).
Preferred sources: ACR 2020 reproductive health guideline (Sammaritano LR; both co-published versions,
PMID 32090480 and the Arthritis Care & Research version — find its PMID and PMCID), EULAR 2017 SLE/APS
women's health (Andreoli, PMID 27457513), EULAR points to consider for antirheumatic drugs before and
during pregnancy and lactation (Götestam Skorpen 2016) and ANY 2023–2026 EULAR update, BSR 2022 guideline
on prescribing drugs in pregnancy and breastfeeding (parts 1 and 2), EULAR 2023 SLE management update,
EULAR 2019 APS in adults (obstetric points at patient level only), CDC US MEC 2024 for contraception in SLE
/ antiphospholipid antibodies, ACR 2022 vaccination guideline (Bass AR, PMID 36597813) for in-utero
biologic exposure, Taiwan sources (Taiwan CDC; Taiwan College of Rheumatology SLE consensus 2026, PMID
41546150 — abstract only so far).

### steroid — 長期使用類固醇：骨頭以外 (systemic glucocorticoids for rheumatic diseases)
The common page will get a card on what to watch for besides bone. Collect patient-level facts and
behaviours that guidelines state:
- adverse effects to know about and monitor: blood glucose / diabetes, blood pressure, weight gain and
  fluid retention, lipids, eyes (cataract, glaucoma / eye pressure), infection (the infection page covers
  details — record only the general point), mood, sleep and other neuropsychiatric effects, skin /
  bruising, stomach (with NSAIDs) — record exactly which effects each source names and which monitoring it
  recommends (e.g. "monitor body weight, blood pressure, peripheral oedema, ... blood glucose and ocular
  pressure"), with population and dose qualifiers;
- that the lowest effective dose for the shortest time is the aim (patient-level phrasing: do not change
  the dose on your own);
- patient education about adverse effects and a written / card record (e.g. a steroid emergency card) if
  a source recommends it;
- not stopping suddenly: adrenal insufficiency — the infection page already has the core sentences;
  record only what adds to them (e.g. the steroid card, tapering is decided by the doctor, symptoms that
  should prompt care);
- whether risks depend on dose and duration (record numbers exactly as stated).
Preferred sources: EULAR 2010 recommendations on monitoring adverse events of low-dose glucocorticoid
therapy (van der Goes MC), EULAR 2013 points to consider for medium- to high-dose glucocorticoids (Duru
N), EULAR 2016 consensus on long-term glucocorticoid harm (Strehl C), EULAR 2007 glucocorticoid management
recommendations (Hoes JN) if not superseded, ESE/Endocrine Society 2024 glucocorticoid-induced adrenal
insufficiency guideline (PMID 38724043), ACR 2021 RA and EULAR 2022 RA management (glucocorticoid
tapering at patient level), any 2023–2026 EULAR/ACR/BSR glucocorticoid safety statements, diabetes
society statements on glucocorticoid-induced hyperglycaemia (record the society and year), ophthalmology
statements on steroid cataract/glaucoma if peer-reviewed.

### cardio — 心血管風險 (rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases)
The common page will get a card on cardiovascular risk. Collect patient-level facts and behaviours:
- which RMDs carry higher cardiovascular risk (record disease by disease as stated: RA, axSpA, PsA, SLE,
  APS, gout, systemic sclerosis, vasculitis, myositis, Sjögren's, etc.) and how much if stated;
- regular cardiovascular risk assessment (who, how often if stated);
- managing traditional risk factors as in the general population (smoking cessation, blood pressure,
  lipids, diabetes, weight, physical activity, diet) — record any disease-specific targets as stated
  (e.g. blood pressure targets in SLE);
- controlling disease activity as part of lowering cardiovascular risk (record exactly what sources say);
- glucocorticoids and NSAIDs in relation to cardiovascular risk (generic classes; patient-level only);
- drug-specific items (e.g. statins, aspirin, antimalarials, urate-lowering drugs) may be recorded as
  background but marked "drug-specific — not for site text"; extract any generic principle separately;
- symptoms that should prompt care (chest pain, etc.) only if a rheumatology source states them;
- myths if a source addresses them.
Preferred sources: EULAR 2022 recommendations for cardiovascular risk management in RMDs including SLE
and APS (Drosos GC, Ann Rheum Dis 2022), EULAR 2016 update for inflammatory joint disorders (Agca R, Ann
Rheum Dis 2017), ESC 2021 guidelines on CVD prevention (the parts on inflammatory/autoimmune disease),
EULAR 2021 lifestyle (Gwinnutt, PMID 35260387) for generic lifestyle points, any 2023–2026 updates, and
Taiwan data or guidance if found.
