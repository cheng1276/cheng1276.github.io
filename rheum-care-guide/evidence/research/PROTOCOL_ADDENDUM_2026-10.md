## Topic-specific scope (in addition to PROTOCOL.md scope)

### oa — 退化性關節炎 (osteoarthritis of the knee, hip and hand)
Self-management and lifestyle: exercise (types, supervised vs home, tai chi, yoga, aquatic, walking),
weight loss (population restriction, amount if stated), education/self-management programmes, walking
aids (cane), braces/orthoses/splints, footwear/insoles, heat/cold, pacing, work/daily activities, sleep
if stated. Medication-adjacent patient behaviours only if a guideline states them. Myths and
not-recommended items commonly believed in Taiwan: glucosamine, chondroitin, other supplements
(collagen, fish oil, vitamin D for OA), "rest protects the joint / exercise wears the joint out",
"running/stairs cause knee OA" (only if a Tier-1/Tier-2 source addresses it), massage, acupuncture
(record direction and strength exactly). Seek-care statements only if a source states them. Prefer:
ACR/Arthritis Foundation 2019 (Kolasinski SL, PMID 31908163 and co-published version), OARSI 2019,
NICE NG226 (2022), EULAR hand OA 2018 update, EULAR 2018 physical activity, any newer EULAR/OARSI/ACR
update you find (check for 2023–2026 updates), Taiwan or Asian guidelines.

### osteoporosis — 骨質疏鬆症 (postmenopausal women, men ≥50; GIOP is already on the common page)
Lifestyle and self-care: calcium (food first vs supplements, amounts if stated), vitamin D (amounts if
stated, sunlight), protein, exercise (weight-bearing, resistance, balance; safe movement after
vertebral fracture), fall prevention (home hazards, footwear, vision, medication review, balance
training), smoking, alcohol (amounts as stated), caffeine/other diet if stated, who should have bone
density testing (patient-level statement only), adherence/medication-adjacent patient behaviours that
guidelines state (e.g. not stopping a treatment without a plan, dental hygiene / dental check before
or during antiresorptives, reporting new back pain or height loss), seek-care statements if sourced.
Myths: bone broth for calcium (only with a peer-reviewed source), "no symptoms so no treatment",
"only women get it", "calcium alone is enough", "exercise is dangerous with osteoporosis", "calcium
supplements cause kidney stones" (record what sources actually say, both directions). Prefer: BHOF 2022,
NOGG 2022 (Gregson CL, Arch Osteoporos 2022;17:58, PMID 35378630), AACE 2020, Endocrine Society, IOF,
World Falls Guidelines 2022 (Montero-Odasso M, Age Ageing), USPSTF if current, Taiwan Osteoporosis
Association guideline / Taiwanese consensus (record whether PubMed-indexed and what you could read).

### labs — 健檢報告異常 (abnormal health-check results that bring people to rheumatology)
Scope is wider than lifestyle here: what a positive antinuclear antibody (ANA) test, a positive
rheumatoid factor (RF) or anti-CCP, and a high serum uric acid without gout symptoms mean for a lay
reader, at the level of published facts. Collect: prevalence of ANA positivity in healthy people by
titre (e.g. Tan EM 1997, PMID 9324014; Satoh M 2012 NHANES; Asian/Taiwanese data if any), that ANA
alone does not diagnose SLE (e.g. EULAR/ACR 2019 SLE classification criteria use ANA ≥1:80 as an entry
criterion only; ACR position statements on ANA testing; ICAP), ANA patterns only if a source explains
them for clinicians in a way usable for lay text, RF/anti-CCP prevalence in healthy and older people
and their role in the 2010 ACR/EULAR RA classification criteria (joint involvement required),
"Choosing Wisely"-type professional society statements on ANA/RF testing (record the society and
whether peer-reviewed), asymptomatic hyperuricaemia: what ACR 2020 says (direction and strength),
EULAR, Japanese guideline, Taiwan 2018 consensus (abstract or full text), lifestyle for high uric acid
(do not duplicate the gout page; record only what is specific to asymptomatic hyperuricaemia), and when
a person with an abnormal result should see a doctor/rheumatologist (only if a source states it).
Never record individual diagnostic algorithms or drug dosing.

### infection — 治療期間防感染 (people taking glucocorticoids, conventional/biologic/targeted DMARDs or
immunosuppressants for rheumatic diseases)
Patient-level behaviours and facts that guidelines state: screening before treatment (latent TB, HBV,
HCV, HIV, others as stated) and why, completing latent-TB treatment, HBV status guiding antiviral
prophylaxis or monitoring (patient behaviour: tell the doctor your hepatitis B status, keep follow-up
blood tests, do not stop antiviral prophylaxis on your own — only if stated), Pneumocystis prophylaxis
as a medication-adjacent behaviour if stated, herpes zoster, vaccines (the common page already covers
general vaccine principles and live-vaccine deferral; record only what adds to it), recognising
infection and contacting the care team (fever etc. — only if a source states it), what to do with
antirheumatic drugs during an infection (only patient-level statements that a guideline makes, e.g.
"contact your doctor"), food safety / travel / other everyday precautions only if a rheumatology or
infectious-disease guideline states them for this population. Taiwan matters a lot here (HBV endemic,
TB intermediate burden): look for Taiwan CDC TB guidelines (結核病診治指引), Taiwan Rheumatology
Association recommendations on TB/HBV screening for biologics, Taiwan Association for the Study of the
Liver (TASL) HBV guidance, APASL/EASL/AASLD statements on HBV reactivation with immunosuppression. Prefer
EULAR 2022 (Fragoulis), ACR 2022 vaccination, EULAR 2019 vaccination update, any 2023–2026 updates.
