# Evidence file: Long-term glucocorticoids — what to know and watch for besides bone (長期使用類固醇：骨頭以外) — people taking systemic glucocorticoids for rheumatic diseases

Prepared under /home/claude/cheng1276.github.io/rheum-care-guide/evidence/research/PROTOCOL.md and /home/claude/newpages2/PROTOCOL_ADDENDUM.md (topic "steroid"). Session date: 2026-10-11. Every quote below was read in this session. This file contains no patient-facing Chinese text. Chinese text appears only inside verbatim quotes from Taiwanese sources.

## Reading notes (please read before using any row)

- **What this file adds to the site.** The site already says (infection page): higher dose and longer use are linked to serious infection, and doctors aim for the lowest effective dose for the shortest time (ACR 2021); do not miss doses or stop suddenly (adrenal crisis); possible temporary stress dosing when ill (ask in advance); vomiting or diarrhoea on or recently off glucocorticoids → seek care; Pneumocystis prophylaxis with high doses. The common page covers bone (calcium, vitamin D, exercise, falls, fracture-risk assessment). Those points are **not** re-recorded here except where a source adds something (marked "adds to infection page"). New material: which other adverse effects sources name (glucose, blood pressure, weight/oedema, lipids, eyes, mood/sleep, skin, muscle, stomach), what monitoring they recommend, patient education and written cards, tapering decided and supervised by the doctor, symptoms during tapering (withdrawal syndrome vs adrenal insufficiency vs flare), the period after stopping, and lifestyle measures with their evidence limits.
- **Access methods.**
  - "Full text (PMC)" = read with get_full_text_article and searched with python: S6 (ESE/ES 2024), S7 (ACR 2021 RA), S11 (BSR 2026 SLE), S16 (Taiwan RA comorbidity consensus), S17 (Taiwan uveitis consensus), S21 (Sports Med review). The PMC record for S1 (PMC2095301) returned an empty text, so S1 was read on the publisher page.
  - "Full text (WebFetch)" = publisher or repository page/PDF read through WebFetch: S1, S2, S4, S8 (ard.bmj.com article pages); S3 (Clin Exp Rheumatol "free to view" PDF); S5 (publisher PDF on the Utrecht University repository); S9 (publisher PDF, CC BY-NC-ND, Leiden University repository); S12 (publisher PDF, CC BY-NC-ND, read from a copy hosted on an industry website — see Section E); S13 (Diabetes UK PDF); S15 (College of Optometrists page, public part only); S18 (Taiwan FDA e-paper pages); S19 (NHS England page). WebFetch passes text through a summarising model that caps each quote at about 125 characters, so longer sentences were requested as consecutive segments and then **checked in "MATCH" passes** (the tool was given the assembled string and asked for any character difference). From early in the session, every MATCH pass contained one deliberately altered control string; **the checker flagged the altered string in all 18 such passes** (S1 ×2, S2 ×2, S3 ×2, S4, S5, S8, S9 ×2, S12, S13, S15, S18 ×2, S19, S6 [JCEM page]). Early S4 passes had no control, so S4 was re-checked with a control (flagged). Quotes that rest on a single WebFetch read (not MATCH-checked) are marked "single read" in Notes and listed in Section E.
  - "Abstract" = PubMed abstract via get_article_metadata: S10, S14, S20, S22.
- **Dropped-word / glued-word pitfall.** No sentence relied on here has a glued word unless the Notes say how its meaning was confirmed. Glued words seen and avoided: S7 ("Bothandrecommendations", "recommendationlonger-term", "recommendationsthe", "toaim", "andconsider"); S11 ("19%10%", "17%0%" — a dropped "vs"); S16 ("dyslipidemiaand", "risk,and", "comorbiditiesresulting" — citation-strip joins); S17 ("uveitistopical", "administeredthese", "administeredperiocular" — a dropped "via"). In S6's "Summary of Recommendations" the numbered sub-items of R 2.7 and R 2.9 were empty; R 2.9's items were read in the body text.
- **Dose and population qualifiers.** S2/S3 cover low dose (≤7.5 mg prednisone or equivalent daily); S4 covers medium/high dose (>7.5 mg but ≤100 mg prednisone equivalent daily); S5 covers long-term therapy; S6 covers anyone on systemic glucocorticoids (endocrine guideline, not rheumatology); S7/S8/S9/S10/S16 cover RA; S11 SLE; S12 polymyalgia rheumatica, giant cell arteritis and Takayasu arteritis (indirect for the site's diseases); S13 is written mainly for inpatient care (UK). Dose and duration numbers are recorded only as risk or scope descriptors, as the addendum asks; **they are clinician-level and are not dosing instructions**. Drug-specific statements are marked "drug-specific — not for site text".
- **Old recommendations.** The EULAR glucocorticoid-safety documents are old: S1 (2007), S2 (2010), S4 (2013), S5 (2016). Searches of PubMed (2017–2026) found **no newer EULAR, ACR or BSR glucocorticoid-safety recommendation set**; none of the four is formally superseded. S4 describes S1 as "mainly based on evidence and experience with low-dose GC therapy". Newer documents touch the same points within disease guidelines (S9 RA 2025, S11 SLE 2026, S12 PMR/LVV 2025) and for the adrenal axis and tapering (S6, 2024). S11 states that international guidance on glucocorticoid dosing and tapering was being developed but not yet published (C69, Notes).
- **Confidence**: High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session. Medium = Tier-2, Tier-1 abstract only, an example/illustrative item in a Tier-1-related paper, a partial or professional-body web guideline, a national-agency web communication, a single-read WebFetch quote, or a Tier-1 statement for an indirect population (S12: PMR/GCA/TAK). Low = secondary source, agency consumer communication written by non-specialists, or indirect evidence (other diseases, other routes of administration, outcome-measurement consensus).
- **Plain-language takeaways** marked "(inference)" go one step beyond the source wording. Editors should keep them only if the inference is acceptable.
- **Taiwanese quotes** are reproduced in the original Chinese; English in the Claim column is my translation for editors.
