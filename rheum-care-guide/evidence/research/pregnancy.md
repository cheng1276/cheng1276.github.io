## Reading notes

How to read this file (caveats that apply throughout):
- "PMC full text" = text returned by the PubMed MCP `get_full_text_article` tool. Reference citations are stripped and appear as `[]`, `()`, `(,)` or `(–)`, or vanish and glue two words together (e.g. "respectively.In", "few,showing"). Those gaps are kept verbatim inside quotes and are not missing words. Words that were italic or bold in the original are sometimes DROPPED (the protocol's "against" pitfall). Every PMC quote used here was checked for glued words. Where a formatted word was missing, the meaning was confirmed from an independent copy, and the Notes say how.
- "WebFetch" = a publisher page or repository PDF read through the WebFetch tool, which passes pages through a summarising model and limits each verbatim quote to about 125 characters. Long sentences were requested as numbered consecutive segments and joined here. Line-break hyphen artefacts are kept as delivered (e.g. "aPL- positive", "pregnancy- compatible"). Where a WebFetch quote was retrieved only once, the Notes say "single retrieval". Where it was retrieved two or more times with identical wording, the Notes say "consistent across N fetches". WebFetch output that the tool itself labelled as a paraphrase was NOT used as a quote.
- The 2020 ACR reproductive health guideline (S1) has no PMC copy. It was read as the published Arthritis & Rheumatology PDF in the University of Michigan "Deep Blue" repository, through WebFetch only. About journal pages 529 to 547 were visible. The rest of the lactation section, the rest of Table 4 and later pages were not visible.
- The 2024 EULAR update on antirheumatic drugs in reproduction, pregnancy and lactation (S4) supersedes the 2016 EULAR points to consider (S7). Its full text could NOT be read (publisher site, ScienceDirect and repository PDF all failed). Only its PubMed abstract (Medium) and a German-language summary written by two of its task-force members (S5, PMC full text; Low, translated by me) were read. The English wording of its overarching principles and recommendations is therefore unverified.
- Confidence: High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session (PMC, or WebFetch consistent across 2 or more fetches). Medium = Tier 2, Tier-1 abstract only, or Tier-1 text from a single WebFetch retrieval. Low = secondary, translated, regulatory excerpt, or indirect.

Marking conventions (from the addendum; the site will not name individual drugs on this page):
- "drug-specific — not for site text" = names an individual medicine. Recorded as background only. The generic principle is recorded in a separate claim.
- "drug class — physician to decide" = names a class only (e.g. biologics, NSAIDs, glucocorticoids), no individual medicine.
- "method class" = a contraceptive method class (IUD, implant, progestin-only pill, estrogen-containing methods, etc.). Where the method name also contains a hormone name (levonorgestrel IUD, DMPA), the Notes say so.
- "medication-adjacent" = a patient behaviour message about medicines stated by a guideline. No prescription doses are recorded. Where a quote contained a dose, the dose is replaced by "(…)" and the Notes say so.

What the site already says (from the addendum), and how this file relates:
- The SLE card already covers: early family planning; disease activity in the 6–12 months before conception; conceiving when quiet and on suitable medicines; contraception with active disease or fetus-harming medicines; pre-pregnancy counselling and re-checking anti-Ro/La; age, smoking and alcohol. Claims that restate these are marked "Already on SLE card" and are recorded only so this page can cite them for all RMDs or extend them.
- New material for an all-RMD page: guidance for every RMD (ACR 2020, BSR 2022, EULAR 2024), a stated 6-month quiescence period (ACR), men's medicines, breastfeeding, antibody testing beyond SLE (anti-Ro/La in Sjögren's, systemic sclerosis, RA), post-partum flare, fertility preservation, assisted reproduction, menopause hormone therapy, infant live vaccines after biologic exposure in the womb (incl. BCG), Taiwan's BCG schedule, and myths.

Headline findings (details and quotes below):
1. Most people with RMDs can have a successful pregnancy with planning. Guidelines ask clinicians to move "from caution against pregnancy towards embracement of pregnancy" (S3).
2. Plan conception for a period of quiescence/low activity on pregnancy-compatible medicines. ACR: "6 months of stable inactive or low-level disease is most often suggested" (S1). Pregnancy-incompatible medicines are switched in advance, and the new medicine is observed for "usually a minimum of several months" (S1, S8/S9).
3. Stopping all medicines is not the aim. Unnecessary withdrawal risks flares and poor disease control (S8/S9). Disease control has priority in severe disease (S9).
4. Contraception (method class): effective methods are preferred, with IUDs/implants the most effective. Estrogen-containing methods are strongly recommended against in antiphospholipid-antibody-positive women. Emergency contraception should be discussed with everyone (S1, S3).
5. Men: paternal exposure to most medicines has not been convincingly linked to harm, and men should be reassured (S9, S10). A few medicines must be stopped before conception (S1). Fertility preservation applies to gonadotoxic treatment (S1, S3, S8).
6. Anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB should be tested once before or early in pregnancy in SLE/SLE-like disease, Sjögren's, systemic sclerosis and RA (ACR, strong). Antiphospholipid antibodies should be tested in SLE (S1).
7. Post-partum flares are common (S1, S19, S20). BSR asks for specialist review through the post-partum period (6 months in its executive summary vs four months in its full guideline: conflict).
8. Breastfeeding has numerous benefits (S1). Many, not all, antirheumatic medicines are considered compatible (S4 abstract, S8/S9).
9. Infants exposed to biologics in the womb: live vaccines may need re-timing, and the pregnant patient should tell the paediatrician before delivery (S11). BCG is the vaccine with the clearest harm signal when given early after TNF-inhibitor exposure (S8, S23). Thresholds differ between sources (6 vs 7 vs 12 months). Taiwan gives BCG at 5–8 months (S27/S28). No Taiwan CDC statement on BCG after maternal biologic use was found (Section D).

### E2. Conflicts between sources (all flagged)
1. **Post-partum specialist review period:** BSR part 1 executive summary (S9) says "for 6 months post-partum". BSR part 1 full guideline (S8) says "the four-month post-partum period". BSR part 2 executive summary (S10) says "the 4-month post-partum period". (C70)
