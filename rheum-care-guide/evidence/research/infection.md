Prepared under /home/claude/cheng1276.github.io/rheum-care-guide/evidence/research/PROTOCOL.md and /home/claude/newpages/PROTOCOL_ADDENDUM.md (topic "infection"). Session date: 2026-10-10. Every quote below was read in this session. This file contains no patient-facing Chinese text. Chinese text appears only inside verbatim quotes from Taiwanese sources.

## Reading notes (please read before using any row)

- **Main source (S1, EULAR 2022) was read as the published PDF through WebFetch**, from an open-access copy on the University of Parma repository (air.unipr.it). WebFetch passes pages through a summarising model that refuses long quotes, so the recommendations were requested in short segments. **All S1 recommendation texts, Table 1 levels and every S1 main-text sentence used as a claim were then re-checked in separate "MATCH" passes** (the tool was given the exact string and asked to report any character difference). All matched. The first paraphrased answer about Recommendation 1 gave the wrong levels for its asterisked parts; that was corrected by three later, consistent reads (see Section E). A few short S1 phrases used only as context rest on a single read; they are listed in Section E, item 20.
- **Other WebFetch-derived full texts**: JAK-inhibitor consensus 2024 (S4, University of Geneva repository PDF), BSR 2019 biologic-safety executive summary (S5, OUP page), BSR 2025 csDMARD guideline (S6, OUP page, partial), Taiwan CDC chapter and press release (S14, S15) and EULAR 2019 vaccination (S18, ard.bmj.com page). Key quotes from S4, S5, S6, S14 and S15 were verified by a second read or a MATCH pass. Where a quote rests on a single read, the Notes column says so. **Check WebFetch-derived quotes character by character against the original before publication** (Section E).
- **PMC-extracted quotes (access "full text (PMC)")**: the extraction tool strips citation numbers, which leaves empty brackets such as "()", "(–)" or "[]", and it can glue words together. Brackets are reproduced as extracted. Sentences with glued words were not relied on, or the Notes column explains how the meaning was confirmed (the dropped-"against" pitfall in PROTOCOL.md). Glued words found and avoided: S3 ("Bothandrecommendations", "recommendationlonger-term", "recommendationsthe"), S2 ("againstshould", "withorin", "bDMARDscompared"), S7 ("Use ofDuring Pregnancy"), S8 ("cirrhosiswith"), S25 ("thisdocument"). The PubMed abstract of S1 also contains a glued word ("againstseems"); see C53.
- **Taiwanese quotes** are reproduced in the original Chinese. The English in the Claim column is my translation for editors, not patient text.
- **Confidence**: High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session (including national-agency guideline text). Medium = Tier-2, Tier-1 abstract only, a Tier-1 statement restating another document that was not read, or a WHO recommendation read on a WHO web platform rather than in the guideline itself. Low = a single Tier-3 study (even when reported inside a guideline) or indirect evidence.
- **Medication-adjacent** rows are patient-behaviour messages only. **No drug doses are recorded.** Glucocorticoid dose thresholds (for example for Pneumocystis prophylaxis, TB risk or "stress dosing") were deliberately left out. Durations and monitoring intervals that appear in verbatim quotes are clinician-level details and should not be printed as instructions.
- **Numbering**: C70 and C71 (AASLD 2018) were added after the first numbering pass. They sit at the end of section B4, out of sequence.
- **Plain-language takeaways** marked "(inference)" go one step beyond the source wording, for example turning "patients should be screened" into "tell your doctor your hepatitis B status". Editors should keep them only if the inference is acceptable.
- **Overlap with the common page**: general vaccine principles, live-vaccine deferral, influenza, pneumococcal, recombinant zoster and HPV vaccines are already in common.md B8 (C62–C80, C93). This file records only additions: hepatitis B vaccination for people at risk (C34), varicella immunity and post-exposure advice (C56–C57), and zoster risk with JAK inhibitors (C5, C55).
- **Population caveat**: S1 and S2 cover adults with autoimmune inflammatory rheumatic diseases (AIIRD), which includes RA, axSpA, SLE and Sjögren's. Gout, chronic urticaria, OA and osteoporosis are not AIIRD. Glucocorticoid-related rows (C4, C64, C65) apply to anyone on long-term glucocorticoids.
- Abbreviations: AIIRD = autoimmune inflammatory rheumatic diseases; bDMARD/tsDMARD/csDMARD = biologic/targeted synthetic/conventional synthetic DMARD; IS = immunosuppressant; GC = glucocorticoid; LTBI = latent TB infection; IGRA = interferon-gamma release assay; TST = tuberculin skin test; CXR = chest X-ray; HBsAg = hepatitis B surface antigen; anti-HBc = hepatitis B core antibody; HBVr = HBV reactivation; NUC = nucleos(t)ide analogue; PCP/PJP = Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia; VZV = varicella zoster virus; PEP = post-exposure prophylaxis; JAKi = JAK inhibitor; TNFi = TNF inhibitor; DOPT = directly observed preventive therapy (都治); LoE = level of evidence; GoR/SoR = grade/strength of recommendation; LoA = level of agreement (S1: 0–10 scale); SOA/SoA = strength of agreement (BSR).

## E. Could not verify / open questions / conflicts

1. **"Do not stop antiviral prophylaxis on your own": not stated in any source read.** The nearest statements are EULAR (S1) on continuing for "least 6–12 months" after antirheumatic treatment stops (with no formal recommendation), APASL (S8) on stopping 6 months after immunosuppression "under the guidance of a hepatologist", APASL's description of reactivation hepatitis on withdrawal of immunosuppression, and AASLD 2018 (S25): "Patients should be monitored for up to 12 months after cessation of anti-HBV therapy." Together these support "keep follow-up blood tests, and stopping is a specialist decision", but not a verbatim "do not stop on your own". A Taiwanese single-centre study (Lan TY et al., Clin Exp Rheumatol 2024;42(9):1830–1837; PMID 38855955; abstract read) reported hepatitis flares after stopping antivirals in HBsAg-positive rheumatic patients on rituximab. That is Tier 3 and was **not used as a claim**; it is a lead only.
2. **"Tell your doctor your hepatitis B status": not stated as a patient instruction in any source read.** The sources say everyone should be screened and that treatment is guided by HBV status (C31–C32). The patient wording is an inference.
3. **Specific infection warning symptoms** (fever thresholds, cough, urinary symptoms and so on) for patients on these drugs were **not found** in any accessible rheumatology guideline. S1 only says patients should be educated to recognise signs and seek attention. S17 gives fever and infection needing antibiotics as examples of "minor stress" in the steroid context. S14 mentions monitoring TB symptoms without listing them. Same gap as sle.md C162.
4. **Pausing csDMARD, bDMARD or tsDMARD during a minor infection**: no patient-level rule found. BSR 2019 covers serious infection only (clinician-directed). JAKi 2024 lists the question in its research agenda. BSR 2025 csDMARD infection-management sections were not accessible (the page text ends inside Table 1).
5. **CONFLICT: scope of HIV screening.** EULAR 2022: recommended before bDMARDs, considered before others (LoE 5, GoR D). BSR 2025: all adults starting a csDMARD (except HCQ/mepacrine), GRADE 1B. JAKi 2024: "in high-risk populations". Suggested editorial line: "Your doctor may offer an HIV test before treatment."
6. **DIFFERENCE: monitoring interval for resolved HBV.** EULAR: "regular (eg, every 3–6 months)". AGA 2025: "1- to 3-month intervals" including viral load (abstract). APASL: liver tests every 12 weeks in specified low/moderate-risk groups. TRA 2012 (via S12): monthly for 3 months, then every 3 months. ACR 2021: "frequent monitoring" (no interval in text read). Patient text should say only "regular blood tests as scheduled".
7. **DIFFERENCE: prophylaxis versus monitoring in resolved HBV.** ACR 2021 conditionally prefers monitoring alone for non-rituximab b/tsDMARDs. TRA 2012 (secondary) "recommended" prophylaxis for inactive carriers and resolved infection but described cost-driven practice of treating only on HBV DNA or HBsAg positivity. APASL recommends pre-emptive NUCs for anti-HBc-positive patients in its high-risk group, and in its moderate-risk group if fibrosis is advanced. All agree on rituximab as high risk.
8. **DIFFERENCE: antiviral duration after immunosuppression stops.** EULAR: about 6–12 months (no formal recommendation; the source omits "at" in "for least"; possibly longer with rituximab). APASL: consider stopping at 6 months for specified groups. APASL also says "The AASLD, AGA and EASL recommend antiviral treatment should be continued for at least 6 months after discontinuation of IST and at least 12 months for B cell-depleting agents". The **AASLD part is confirmed** by S25 ("at least 6 months (or for at least 12 months for patients receiving anti-CD20 therapies)"). The AGA and EASL parts were **not verified** (AGA 2025 abstract only; EASL 2025 reactivation section not accessible).
8a. **DIFFERENCE IN EMPHASIS: anti-HBs.** S2 (Tier 2): anti-HBs "appear to be protective". APASL: "some protection", but insufficient evidence for clinical decisions. AASLD 2018: "The presence of anti-HBs does not prevent HBV reactivation". Safe patient message: having anti-HBs does not remove the need for monitoring (M13).
9. **TB re-screening emphasis.** EULAR: "could be considered". BSR 2025: "not generally indicated but may be considered" with specific risk factors. That BSR sentence sits under the HBV/HCV/HIV recommendation, so it may not refer to TB. Compatible but different emphasis.
10. **BSR 2025 Rec 6 internal wording discrepancy**: recommendations table, "increased risk for latent tuberculosis"; evidence section, "increased risk for tuberculosis exposure". Same grade and SoA.
11. **EULAR 2022 Rec 1 levels**: the first WebFetch answer (a paraphrase) said the asterisked parts carried LoE 2b / GoR B. Three later direct reads consistently gave "2b; 5*" and "B; D*" (asterisked parts LoE 5 / GoR D). The later values are recorded.
12. **JAKi 2024 VI.1**: the first read dropped "and" before "herpes zoster"; the MATCH pass restored it. Item III.1 table levels (2b/B, *4/D; LoA 9.7 ± 0.6) differ from the body text (2b/5, B/D; LoA 9.73).
13. **WHO TB recommendation (S16)**: read on the WHO TB Knowledge Sharing Platform (2020 guideline) and the BIGG/PAHO database. The certainty label differs ("very_low_to_low" vs "Very low") and the final word differs ("LTBI" vs "latent tuberculosis infection (LTBI)"). The 2024 second edition (ISBN 978-92-4-009619-6) could not be opened, so its wording is unverified.
14. **Taiwan CDC LTBI chapter (S14)**: book title and edition not printed in the accessible text; probably a later edition of S13 than the 7th (page numbers differ), not confirmed. In §10.3.5 the sentence recommending DOPT opens with "由於該族群非疾病管制署公費補助對象，故無法享有公費 IGRA 檢驗及就醫部分負擔的補助". This seems to say the immune-rheumatic group is not CDC-subsidised, which seems to sit uneasily with §10.2.1 listing anti-TNF users as a policy target group. Not resolved. Cost statements were not used.
15. **TASL 2019 (S11)**: only the abstract was read. Its recommendations for patients on immunosuppression are unknown. **EASL 2025 (S10)** and **AGA 2025 (S9)**: reactivation recommendations not read in full. **TCR SLE 2026 (S21)** and **TRA/TAPSI PsA 2020 (S22)**: infection statements unknown.
16. **2012 TRA TB and HBV consensus documents**: cited by S12 but not found in PubMed. Likely published outside PubMed-indexed journals; not read. Treat C19 and C45 as secondary and possibly outdated.
17. **BSR 2019 biologic safety guideline (S5)**: a 2025 BSR scope (S24) shows an update is in progress. Zoster and varicella vaccination items in S5 reflect the live-vaccine era and were not used. BSR "Patients who do not have a positive history of varicella zoster (chickenpox) infection should have a varicella zoster virus antibody test…" carries a grade (2C, SOA 98%) that covers a following vaccination clause, so it was not used. The full S5 guideline page was truncated mid-HBV section.
18. **EULAR 2019 vaccination Rec 4 (S18)**: one verbatim read. The second read refused verbatim quoting but confirmed the content and the em dash joining the two parts. Whether Taiwan counts as an "endemic country" for this purpose is not stated by the source; it is my inference, so do not print it as a source statement.
19. **Glued or dropped words handled**: S1 abstract "againstseems" (italic organism name dropped; Table 1 confirms "Prophylaxis against PCP"; no direction issue). S2 "withorin" (pathogen names dropped; C67 confirms Listeria and Salmonella; exact dropped wording unknown). S1 body text prints "anti-HBcorepositive" and "HBVDNA" (reproduced as printed). S8 "cirrhosiswith" (that sentence was quoted only up to the glued word). S25 "thisdocument" in the Introduction (an italic word, probably "guidance", dropped; it does not affect any quoted statement).
20. **Single-read quotes** (no second read or MATCH pass): S1 OAP A second sentence ("cornerstone"); S1 rituximab "might be longer" phrase; S1 methods phrases ("the Oxford Evidence Based Medicine categorisation was followed"; "from 0 (=no agreement) to 10 (=full agreement)"; "this is covered by another set of EULAR recommendations"); S5 first sentence of C61; S5 full-guideline Perez-Alvarez passage (C47); S13 §1.3.1 phrase; S14 §10.3.2 sentences; S14 §10.2.1 full list item; S16 WHO recommendation (BIGG wording consistent); S23 ACR summary phrase; M10 first sentence (assembled from segments). **All WebFetch-derived quotes should be checked against the original documents before publication.**
21. **Food safety**: no guideline read lists specific foods to avoid for this population. "Raw eggs and poultry" comes from S2's description of one study's leaflet. No Taiwan-specific food-safety guidance for immunosuppressed people was found.
22. **COVID-19** was not researched. S1 has no COVID-19 recommendation, and its informing SLR (S2) excluded COVID-19 studies. The common page also excludes it.
23. **Taiwan NHI reimbursement conditions** (TB and HBV screening requirements for biologics) were not checked.
24. **APLAR 2024 HBV figure not used**: S7 reports "an estimated HBV reactivation rate of 2.0% (95% CI 0.01 to 0.04)", mixing a percentage with a proportion-scale CI. It was recorded here and not used as a claim.
