## Reading notes (please read before using any row)

- **PMC-extracted quotes (access "full text (PMC)")**: the extraction tool removes citation numbers, which leaves empty brackets such as "()" or "(,)". Those brackets are reproduced exactly as extracted. It also drops formatted words. In the 2020 ACR guideline it dropped "against" from several statements, leaving glued words such as "recommendedfor". Wherever this matters, the Notes column says how the true direction was confirmed.
- **WebFetch-derived quotes (access "full text via WebFetch")**: these pages (publisher HTML, repository PDFs, NICE PDF, Malaysian CPG PDF) were read through a fetch tool that passes the page through a summarising model. That model sometimes refuses quotes longer than about 125 characters. To limit the risk of altered wording, the long key quotes were re-requested in consecutive short segments and checked for consistency: EULAR 2016 principles B and C, NICE 1.2.1/1.3.5/1.4.1/1.4.2, and Malaysian Rec 5. BSR 2017 Recs I, II, IX and X were cross-checked against two separate repository files (the author-accepted manuscript and the executive summary). Even so, **check WebFetch-derived quotes character by character against the original PDF before publication** (see Section E).
- **Confidence**: High = explicit in Tier-1 full text read in this session. Medium = Tier-2, or Tier-1 abstract only, or a Tier-1 sentence whose location in the document could not be identified. Low = secondary source, indirect evidence, or **any Tier-3 single study**. A Tier-3 finding rated Low is quoted accurately but sits in the lowest evidence tier.
- **Medication-adjacent** rows are patient-behaviour messages only. No prescription-drug dosing is recorded.
- "SU" or "sUA" means serum urate / serum uric acid. "ULT" means urate-lowering therapy.

### Conflict summary (flagged in the rows above)

1. **Specific diet advice vs "no specific diet proven"**: ACR, EULAR, BSR, China and Malaysia give food-specific advice (C13–C30, C42–C45). NICE says there is not enough evidence that any specific diet prevents flares or lowers urate (C08). Both positions are recorded.
2. **Wine**: wine was not associated with incident gout in men (C23), and EULAR 2016 emphasises beer and spirits (C16). In people with gout, wine was associated with recurrent attacks (C22), and Malaysia says limit all types (C21).
3. **Cherries and dairy**: ACR made no recommendation because certainty was low or very low (C53). EULAR, BSR and China 2024 encourage them (C49–C51, C59–C61).
4. **Vitamin C**: ACR is conditionally against (C63). BSR Rec XIX notes a "weak uricosuric effect" but its text calls the effect clinically insufficient (C65). S24 reports that CPGs disagree.
5. **Diet soft drinks**: the ACR text cites NHANES linking *artificially sweetened* carbonated beverages with higher SU (C48). Choi 2008 found diet soft drinks not associated with incident gout (C46).
6. **Urine alkalinisation (physician-directed; not self-care)**: ACR is conditionally against for uricosuric users (direction confirmed via S2). China 2024 suggests it (S9 Recs 12–14). Recorded for awareness only.
7. **Omega-3 fish**: Malaysia exempts omega-3-rich fish from purine limitation (C30). Other guidelines that were read do not make this exception.

## E. Could not verify / open questions

1. **Taiwan consensus full text (S8)**: its lifestyle content (soy/legumes/tofu/mushrooms, water volume, alcohol) is unknown. Please obtain the PDF (Wiley, Int J Rheum Dis 2018;21:772-787) and the underlying Taiwan Guideline (likely in Chinese) before publishing Taiwan-specific statements.
2. **APLAR 2021 (S7)**: the abstract says there are 5 non-pharmacologic recommendation statements and 3 overarching principles, but their wording, certainty and strength could not be read (publisher HTTP 403). This guideline matters for an Asian audience; please obtain the full text.
3. **Japanese Guideline for the Management of Hyperuricemia and Gout (3rd ed.)**: not accessed. Two secondary mentions only: S36 says counselling "following the guideline" included "drink sufficient water" (no volume stated in the abstract). S37 states: "These values were close to the 400 mg/day recommended in the Japanese Guideline for the management of Hyperuricemia and Gout." Treat the 400 mg/day purine figure as unverified.
4. **Continuing established ULT during a flare**: supported only by BSR Rec I (C94) and the Malaysian algorithm (C95). Not found in ACR 2020 (PMC full text read, plus co-published PDF via WebFetch), NICE NG219 or EULAR 2016 (both checked via WebFetch). The Korean guideline addresses *initiating* ULT during flares, not continuing it.
5. **Rest and elevation**: explicit only in BSR Rec II (C88) among guidelines read. S24 reports it as a CPG consensus but the cited CPGs cannot be identified (references stripped).
6. **Fluid volume**: no guideline read recommends a fluid volume for all gout patients. The only volume found (>2 L/day) is BSR's, for people with past urolithiasis (LoE IV; SOR 57%). No PubMed-indexed study of water intake and gout flares, or of dehydration as a trigger, was found. A narrative review asserting dehydration and starvation as triggers (Kakutani-Hatayama 2015, Am J Lifestyle Med; PMID 30202351) was **excluded** as a narrative review.
7. **Fasting as a trigger**: no guideline statement found; Tier-3 evidence is mixed (C107–C109).
8. **Organ meats (offal) and mushrooms**: no source read in this session names them specifically. BSR (checked) and ACR refer only to "high purine foods" / "purine intake". EULAR refers to "meat and seafood".
9. **EULAR 2016 strengths**: LoE, SoR and LoA for principles A–C and Rec 1 sit in tables not visible in accessible text. Recorded as "not found in accessible text". The text states principle B was "mainly based on expert opinion".
10. **BSR "SOR" definition**: not found in accessible text. Values are printed as percentages with ranges (e.g., 89%, range 54-100). The certainty of the BSR Rec XIX grading was not retrieved.
11. **ACR certainty-of-evidence levels** (Low / Very low) were read from tables of the co-published PDF via a summarising fetch tool. They should be checked against the ACR tables (Arthritis Care Res 2020;72:744-760, Tables 6–8, or the co-published version).
12. **ACR statement on artificially sweetened beverages (C48)** conflicts with Choi 2008. The NHANES source cited by ACR (reference 101 in the co-published version) was not read.
13. **Glued-word pitfalls observed in the S1 PMC text** (all directions confirmed via S2 where used): vitamin C ("recommendedfor"), low-dose aspirin ("recommendedfor"), fenofibrate ("recommendedfor"), urine alkalinisation ("recommendedfor"), checking urinary uric acid ("recommendfor"), ULT after a first flare ("recommendedin"; S2: "we conditionally recommend against initiating ULT over no ULT, with the following exceptions"), ULT in asymptomatic hyperuricaemia ("recommendedin"; S2 confirms "against"), universal HLA-B*5801 testing ("recommendedin"), pegloticase for mild disease ("recommendedfor"), and the strength-definition sentence ("rated asorrecommendations"). The prescribing items are not recorded as claims because they are not patient-behaviour messages.
14. **WebFetch-derived quotes** (S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S12) were extracted by a summarising model. Key long quotes were segment-verified or cross-file-verified (see Reading notes). Before publication, check each quoted sentence against the original PDF.
15. **Minor source-text anomalies (not used, or quoted [sic])**:
    - ACR "those who do did not drink heavily" (typo; not quoted).
    - Cochrane 2021 "found no high-quality that supported" (missing word; quoted [sic] in C64).
    - Duan 2022 "it is prudent that our findings … were extended to patients with hyperuricemia or gout" (meaning ambiguous; not used).
