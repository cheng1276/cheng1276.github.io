- CALCIUM AMOUNT (OP-2.2) AND TAIWANESE REFERENCE INTAKES (physician's decision). The page prints the BHOF figures, which Osteoporosis Canada's RDA matches (1,000 mg for men 50–70 [Canada: 51–70]; 1,200 mg for women ≥51 and men ≥71). They sit inside the common page's 1,000–1,200 mg range (ACR 2022), but they differ from the Taiwanese figures in both directions. (a) TOA 2021, re-read verbatim by the fact-check: 「50 歲以上成人每日至少需攝取飲食鈣量 1200 毫克(包括鈣片補充劑量)和維生素 D 800 至 1000 國際單位」. The page's 1,000 mg for men 50–70 is therefore lower than TOA 2021. (b) HPA 2014 adult DRI: 「成人則為1000 mg」. The page's 1,200 mg for women ≥51 is therefore higher. The HPA DRI page was robots-blocked, so the current DRI edition (and whether it has a separate band for people over 50) was not checked. Other targets: NOGG minimum 700 mg; ESCEO/IOF 800–1,000 mg; AACE 1,200 mg (women ≥50); Asia-Pacific men 1,000–1,200 mg; RACGP 1,300 mg (women >50, men >70). Please compare with TOA 2025 (unreadable here) and the current DRI, and align OP-2.2 with the Taiwanese figures if you prefer. After the fact-check, OP-2.2 ends with BHOF's caveat, verified by the verifier: 'There is no evidence that calcium intakes in excess of recommended amounts confer additional bone benefit' (「超過建議量，未證實對骨骼有額外好處」). TOA 2021's statement that more than 1,200–1,500 mg a day gives no further benefit agrees with it; TOA's added claim of more kidney stones and cardiovascular disease is still not printed, because the cardiovascular part conflicts with NOGG, BHOF, ESCEO/IOF and RACGP.

- BMD TESTING (OP-1.3, OP-1.4), conflict. BHOF and TOA 2021 list women ≥65, men ≥70, and younger postmenopausal women and men aged 50–69/70 with risk factors or a previous (adult-age or fragility) fracture. USPSTF 2025 (abstract) recommends screening women ≥65 and younger postmenopausal women at increased risk (B), but finds the evidence insufficient for men (an I statement, not a recommendation against). RACGP prefers case finding to population screening, and Osteoporosis Canada's targeted approach delays most testing until 70. Common ground used: readers 「可和醫師討論是否做骨密度檢查」; the page does not say everyone in these groups must be tested. The fact-check rated both points OK and called the softening a justified understatement. Labelled 指引說明 because neither BHOF nor TOA grades these items. National Health Insurance reimbursement rules are not in the evidence file and are not mentioned.
- LEFT OUT BECAUSE SOURCES CONFLICT (BRIEF rule 5). (a) Calcium supplements and kidney stones: USPSTF/Kahwati, NOGG, RACGP and ESCEO/IOF say supplements (especially with vitamin D) may modestly increase stones; the Ma 2024 umbrella review says supplements, dietary calcium and vitamin D 'made no difference or even reduced the risk'. The kidney-stone myth was not used for this reason. (b) Calcium and cardiovascular risk (TOA 2021 vs NOGG, BHOF, ESCEO/IOF and RACGP). (c) Back braces after a spine fracture (NOGG: routine bracing 'cannot be recommended'; BHOF: acute treatment includes 'bracing (for 2 to 6 weeks)'). OP-4.3 quotes only the BHOF sentences that follow the bracing clause. (d) Impact and jumping exercise for readers without spine fractures (RACGP Grade B: at least 50 moderate impacts most days; Osteoporosis Canada: impact only if appropriate, safety uncertain at high fracture risk; BHOF lists jumping rope and running among potentially injurious activities yet lists jogging and tennis as weight-bearing; HPA 2014 recommends jumping rope for the general population). Only the SSS precaution for people with spine fractures is printed (OP-4.5). OP-3.1's weight-bearing examples leave out jogging and tennis and match the glossary entry 負重運動 (走路、太極拳、爬樓梯、跳舞).
- LEFT OUT FOR THIN SUPPORT OR SPACE (optional; wording ready). (a) Caffeine and salt: only TOA 2021 (「減少含咖啡因飲品與避免攝取過多鹽份」) and HPA 2014 say this; none of the international guidelines read mention caffeine, and the evidence base was not reviewed. Possible point: 「減少含咖啡因的飲料，也避免吃太鹹。」(台灣骨鬆學會 2021・指引說明). (b) Vegetarian and vegan diets: NOGG observational data (IIa/IIb) with 'inconsistencies in the evidence base'; a sensitive topic in Taiwan. Possible tip: 「研究發現，吃素的人脊椎和髖部骨密度較低，吃全素的人髖部骨折風險較高，部分與鈣和蛋白質的攝取量有關。」(NOGG 2024・研究顯示). (c) Sunlight: RACGP Rec 14 is calibrated for Australia, HPA 2014 gives no duration, and a sun message without limits could clash with the common UV-index card and with SLE photoprotection. (d) Vitamin D does not prevent falls unless the person is at risk of deficiency (WFG key message, High). (e) Footwear: WFG lists it only as an assessment item, and no source says what to wear. (f) Hip protectors (ESCEO/IOF: uncertain, mainly in nursing homes). (g) Practising how to get up from the floor (WFG narrative).

- EXERCISE AFTER A SPINE FRACTURE (OP-4.4, OP-4.T, OP-4.5). NOGG (Strong; Conditional for starting lying down) and SSS ([C]) recommend guided back-strengthening exercise. The Cochrane review (Gibbs 2019, S20) found too little evidence on new fractures, falls or adverse events, and only a small, not clinically important gain in physical performance. The page therefore claims no benefit such as pain relief. OP-4.5 (impact up to brisk walking) is SSS consensus, which SSS calls a precaution against theoretical, unproved risks. Its population is confirmed by the SSS abstract, verified by the fact-check: 'People with vertebral fracture or multiple low trauma fractures should usually exercise only up to an impact equivalent to brisk walking.' After the fact-check the point adds 「一般」 for 'usually' and the examples 「慢跑、跳躍」, taken from SSS's list of moderate-impact exercise ('stamping, jogging, low-level jumping, hopping'), which is SSS's advice for people without spine fractures and is quoted only to explain the term. Its second clause uses NOGG's narrative that people with vertebral fractures 'may need more specific advice and assessment before increasing exercise intensity'. 衝擊性運動 has no glossary entry yet.
